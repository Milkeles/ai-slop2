# Part I — Low Water

The water in Cutter's Bowl was the lowest anyone had seen since 1979. Gran said so, and Gran was usually right about years.

"She remembers the price of bread in 1979," Dobri said. "She can't remember where she put her glasses."

"Old people file things in a different order," Ines said. "Watch the fence. There's a sharp bit."

[[img:bowl]]

They squeezed through the gap in the chain-link. Below them the quarry dropped away in white steps, each terrace a little dirtier than the one above, down to a pool of turquoise water that had shrunk like a puddle in the sun. A wide pale ring of rock showed where the water used to be. The mud on the lowest ledges had dried into plates that curled up at the edges.

Tavi stood at the rim with his hands on his hips. He wasn't allowed to climb. His mum had said it, the physio had said it, and the black brace on his right knee said it every time he bent his leg.

"I'm not climbing," he said. "I'm looking. Looking is legal."

Then he stopped joking. "What's that?"

On a ledge two terraces above the water, right under the end of the old rusted gantry, something curled. A spiral, wide as a dinner plate, ridged like the inside of a shell. In the shadow of the cliff it looked faintly lit, as if someone had traced its lines with a glow pen.

Ines was already counting. "Third terrace, then the slope by the drainpipe, then the crack where the bush is. Forty minutes down. Tavi stays here."

"Tavi does not stay here."

He didn't. He held the back of Dobri's cardigan and came down anyway, swearing at every step. It took them fifty minutes. Ines checked her watch at the bottom and was annoyed about the ten.

Up close it was a fossil, an ammonite, half buried in limestone. Its wavy lines really did glow, the colour of a weak torch under a blanket. Dobri pressed record on Gran's cassette recorder out of habit. Tavi worked a flat stone into the crack beneath it while Ines told him where to push. It came loose with a sound like a tooth being pulled.

All three of them had a hand on it when it came free.

Something clicked inside Ines's head, like a knuckle cracking from the inside. Pressure behind her eyes. For half a second she saw pale ridges, rounded like dunes, and then the quarry again. And a smell, sharp and clean. Hospital hand gel.

"Did you get that?" Tavi was blinking hard. "Bubblegum. Strawberry. Like Pip's stuff."

"Quince," Dobri said. "Like Gran's jam."

Then Tavi laughed, and they all did, a bit too loudly. Old air trapped in the stone, Ines decided. Rock gas.

They called it the Coil, because Tavi said "fossil" sounded like homework.

The next morning, in three different kitchens, things went wrong in small ways.

Ines was making toast when she saw her mum's mug fall. She saw it tip off the counter, hit the tiles and break. She turned and caught it. Only then, two full seconds later, did her mum's elbow knock it off the edge and into her hands.

Her mum stared at her. She was still in her scrubs from the night shift, her hair flat on one side. "Good reflexes," she said. Then: "Have I given you lunch money?"

She always left it under the fruit bowl on Sunday nights. Every week, without fail. Ines found the coins still in her mum's purse. Then she smelled gas, and found the hob turned on with no flame.

"Mum."

"I was going to boil an egg." Hana frowned at the cooker as if it had done it by itself. "Wasn't I?"

Ines looked down at her black wristwatch. She wanted to know exactly how long two seconds was.

[[img:ines]]

Across town, Dobri walked into Gran's kitchen and heard her say, "Boots off, you'll mark the lino." The kitchen was empty. Gran was in the living room. She had said it yesterday, at this door, in exactly that voice.

At breakfast Gran held her hand out across the table. "Pass me the... the..." Her fingers made a small stirring shape. "The thing. For the sugar."

"Spoon," Dobri said.

"Spoon." She laughed at herself. "Eighty years I've been saying spoon."

[[img:dobri]]

Tavi tripped on the top stair at home and should have gone down all fourteen on his face. Instead he turned in the air and landed at the bottom on his feet, light as a cat, without touching the rail. Pip watched from the landing with her mouth open.

"Do it again."

"No," Tavi said, because he didn't know how.

Pip pushed her lips together and blew. Nothing came out but air. "I forgot how to whistle," she said, puzzled. "I could do it yesterday."

The Weighhouse stood at the quarry gate, beside the old weighbridge. It was a small red-brick building with a tin roof and one tall arched window. Inside it was all glass cases and wooden drawers and handwritten labels in brown ink, and it smelled of dust and chalk.

[[img:weighhouse]]

Mr Pell was a tall man with a white beard and a long canvas coat. He took the Coil from Ines without asking and lifted the brass loupe that hung around his neck.

He looked for a long time. His face went the colour of the chalk on his fingers.

"I'll buy it," he said. "Two hundred."

"It's not for sale," Ines said.

Pell set the Coil down on the counter, carefully, as if it were hot. "Put it back in the water. Tonight. It doesn't give." He looked at each of them in turn. "Nothing gives."

Behind him an old slide projector sat on a metal trolley, throwing a square of white light on the wall. When Pell's hand moved, the beam bent toward it. Only a little. Like a plant leaning toward a window.

Dobri's ears went hot. Under the tick of the clock he heard a boy's voice, thin and scared, from somewhere in the room. "Mum? Mum, it's me."

There was no boy. Pell was watching him.

"We're keeping it," Ines said. She picked it up, and they walked out quickly without looking back.

[[img:pell]]

They went back to the Bowl that evening and touched the Coil again. Click, pressure, ridges. Gel, bubblegum, quince. Then again an hour later, because the first time had felt so good.

Tavi unbuckled his brace and laid it on a rock. He stood up, tested his knee, and ran.

He ran the whole rim of the quarry, through the dry grass and down the loose stones, and he didn't slip once. When he came back he wasn't even out of breath. He was laughing.

[[img:tavi]]

Ines practised with her watch. Two seconds. Three. Then five: she saw Dobri sneeze, and five seconds later Dobri sneezed. Something warm ran over her lip. She wiped it and looked at her fingers. Blood.

"Cold water," Dobri said. "Gran says heat goes to the head."

They dunked their faces in the quarry. The cold went through her skull like a bell, and her ears stopped burning.

Then she saw Pip.

She was on the crumbling ledge above them, a small figure in a pink T-shirt who had followed them up the hill. She saw the edge go soft under Pip's trainers. She saw her fall.

"Tavi! The ledge by the bush. Now!"

He didn't ask. He was already moving, up the rock as if it were a staircase, and he got there one heartbeat before the edge gave way. Stones rattled down into the water. Pip hung from his arm, kicking, more surprised than frightened.

"I just wanted to see," she said.

"It saved her," Tavi said at last. "If we'd thrown it back like he said, she'd be in the water."

So they kept it. They agreed to hide it under Dobri's bed in Gran's flat, where nobody would think to look.

Pip skipped ahead of them along the pavement, tripped on nothing at all, and laughed at herself.
