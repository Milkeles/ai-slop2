# Brief: Story Architect

You are designing the story bible for a school workshop project: an original fantasy story presented on a
multi-page website. The full project brief is in `instructions.md` (sections 1–6 matter most to you).
Summary of what matters:

- Idea: a group of friends gain magical powers as their brains gain more wrinkles (gyri) and produce more
  energy. Each friend's power is tied to one brain region; using power has a real cost (nosebleeds, lost
  memories, "smoothing" = folds fading); the cause of the folding must be revealed by the end.
- Final story: 3,500–4,500 words in English, three parts (beginning / middle / end, optional short epilogue).
- 3–4 characters (friends) + optional antagonist; 3–4 clearly different locations, each matters in the plot.
- The website will have 10 generated images and at least 3 interactive 3D models (found online, free
  licence — so pick objects that are likely to exist as free .glb models: e.g. a human brain, a lantern, an
  old radio, a book, a crystal, a tree, a jellyfish, a telescope, a key... not invented gadgets).
- Audience: classmates and a teacher. It should be fun, surprising, emotionally honest, and easy to follow
  when presented aloud. Original: no borrowed characters, settings or names from existing works.

## Produce one Markdown file with exactly these sections

1. **Three alternative directions** (3–4 lines each: logline, tone, core twist) and **the one you choose, with
   the reason**.
2. **Title and logline** (one sentence).
3. **Magic rules** (final): source/cause, how folding feels, region → power map, cost and limits, how it can
   go wrong. Max 12 bullet points.
4. **Characters** (3–4 friends, optional antagonist). For each: name, age, role, personality, motivation, power
   and brain region, key object, inner conflict, arc across the 3 parts, and a **visual line**: one paragraph
   (40–70 words) of concrete physical appearance and clothing that can be pasted into an image prompt
   unchanged (no personality words in it).
5. **Locations** (3–4): name, what it is, why it matters in the plot, which parts it appears in, and a
   **visual line** (40–70 words, concrete, image-prompt ready).
6. **Plot outline**: for each part (I, II, III, + optional epilogue): word budget, 3–5 scenes as numbered beats
   (location, who, what changes), the turning point that ends the part. Mark the twist and the climax.
   Check every beat against the magic rules; list any risk of contradiction and how you avoid it.
7. **Art plan**: one shared **style line** (medium, palette, lighting, mood; 25–40 words, no artist names), and
   a **shot list of exactly 10 images**: filename (lowercase, e.g. `cover.webp`), what it shows, which page
   and where in the text it goes. Include: cover, one portrait per character, one image per location, and
   2–3 key scenes.
8. **3D model plan**: 3–4 real-world objects (findable as free models), each with the page/scene it belongs
   to and why it matters in the story, plus a backup object for each.

Rules: English only. Concrete, not flowery. Do not include any personal data about the user.
